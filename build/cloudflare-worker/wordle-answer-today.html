Today's Wordle Answer Revealed

The answer to Wordle #2612 on May 18, 2026 is LOATH. I burned my fourth guess on LOATH after trying CLOTH and GLOAT first — that silent A in the middle is a real gotcha. The word looks like it should rhyme with "oath" and it does, but that A just sits there doing nothing visually. Four-letter-feeling word with five letters, classic Wordle trap.

Why This Word Fits Today's Puzzle

LOATH is a strong midweek difficulty word. It's common enough in written English — you'll see "loath to admit" or "loath to leave" in news articles — but it's not a word most people say out loud regularly. That disconnect between recognition and active vocabulary is exactly what makes it a good puzzle word. The L and H placement at the edges are tricky because there aren't that many common words with that frame. When I had the pattern _O_TH after guess three, I actually sat there for a minute. LOATH didn't come immediately; I was mentally cycling through GOATH and WOATH before remembering those aren't words.

Meaning and Everyday Use

LOATH means reluctant or unwilling. You're loath to do something you'd rather avoid. It's not quite "hate" — more like a strong preference against. I see it most often in phrases like "loath to criticize" or "loath to concede." The word has a formal register that makes it popular in political and business writing where "doesn't want to" would sound too casual. There's also the related verb "loathe" with an E, meaning to hate intensely, which causes spelling confusion. Today's word is the adjective without the E. If you mix them up, you'll waste a guess.

How to Work Toward the Answer

With L and H locked in as the bookends, the middle three letters need to carry the load. The O and A are fairly common vowels but their adjacent placement (O then A) isn't intuitive — most English words separate them or reverse the order. If you had the O in position two early, you might have chased words like LOFTY, LOBBY, or LORRY before seeing the vowel pair. My advice: when you have two vowels next to each other and one is confirmed, always test the other common vowels in sequence. O and A together isn't rare exactly — think "board" or "coast" — but without the full pattern, it's easy to miss. The TH ending is a huge tell once you see it. If you get to _ _ _ TH, you're usually down to a small set of possibilities.

Best Starter Ideas for This Puzzle

STARE and CRANE both hit common consonants and vowels but miss L and H entirely. For today's word, a starter like SLATE or LEAST gets you the L, which is the most helpful anchor. I personally like LEAST as an opener when I'm feeling like consonant edges might matter — it tests L, S, T, plus two vowels. Another solid choice would be HEART or HALTS for the H. But honestly? No starter is perfect. I used ADIEU today (bad habit, I know) and got the O on guess one, which sent me down a weird path. Extra Tips for Keeping a Streak Alive

When you have three letters correct and one is clearly wrong, don't abandon the good letters to test new ones. I see people do this all the time — they'll have L-O-A-?-H and guess something like LOAMY to test M, when LOATH and LOAMY share the problem that Y doesn't fit. Better to bank the known letters and cycle through consonants in the remaining slot. Also: write down or mentally note words you've already eliminated. On LOATH, if you tried CLOTH, you knew C, L, O, T, H — and that T was wrong. That's valuable. I keep a running list in my phone's notes app. Sounds excessive until you hit day 200 and realize you've forgotten what you guessed yesterday.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does LOATH mean in Wordle?

Reluctant or unwilling. If you're loath to do something, you'd really rather not do it. Common phrase: "loath to admit."

Is LOATH a common Wordle answer?

It's appeared once in the official Wordle answer list — today, May 18, 2026. Before that, no. The related word LOATHE has never been an answer.

Why is the A silent in LOATH?

Historical spelling convention. The word comes from Middle English "loth," and the A was added later by analogy with words like "oath." English spelling is full of these inherited oddities. You just have to memorize them.

What's the difference between LOATH and LOATHE?

LOATH (today's answer, no E) is an adjective meaning reluctant. LOATHE (with E) is a verb meaning to hate intensely. Different words, different pronunciations, easy to confuse in a hurry.
