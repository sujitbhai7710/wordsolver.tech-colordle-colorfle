Quick Tips for Canuckle

Canuckle is Wordle with a Canadian twist — every answer has a connection to Canada. The answer card above shows today's word, puzzle number, and a Canadian fact. It resets at midnight Eastern Time.

Think Canadian first

Words like TOQUE, POUTINE, MAPLE, LOONIE, MOOSE, and SYRUP are all fair game. Once you have a few letters locked in, reach for Canadian vocabulary before generic English words.

Open with ABOUT

ABOUT tests four vowels plus B and T — letters that show up constantly in Canadian answers. It's the best single opener for this game specifically.

Check recent answers

Canuckle rarely repeats within a month. Scan the recent answers grid below before guessing to avoid wasting a try on a word that already came up.

Canuckle FAQ

What time does Canuckle reset?
Midnight Eastern Time, same as Wordle for North American players. If you're in Europe or Asia, the new puzzle appears in your early morning hours.
What does the red feedback mean?
Red is Canuckle's version of Wordle's gray — the letter isn't in the answer. Yellow means it's in the word but in a different spot. Green means correct spot.
How do I browse earlier answers?
The recent answers grid below covers the last 30 days. For the full history, visit the Canuckle archive.

Canuckle Answer Today – May 18, 2026 (#647)

The May 18 Canuckle is INUIT — a word that should feel familiar to anyone who's played this game for more than a week, but still managed to trip me up on letter position. Puzzle #647, and I'm still forgetting how often the Canadian theme leans on Indigenous terms and geography.

Today's Canuckle at a glance

Answer: INUIT

Letter pattern: I-N-U-I-T. Double vowel (I appears twice), no repeated consonants. That second 'I' sitting in position 4 is the silent killer — I burned two guesses on INFER and INPUT before the green tiles locked in. The 'U' in position 3 also throws off standard English vowel placement; your brain wants U near the end or in a "QU" cluster, not floating mid-word.

Canuckle's answer pool runs about 2,300 words versus Wordle's ~2,300, but the thematic constraint means you'll see INUIT, METIS, CREE, and similar terms recycle more often than you'd expect in a standard word list. I track my solves in a spreadsheet (yes, I'm that guy) and this is the third time INUIT has appeared since I started logging in 2022. The repeat frequency is real.

Want to check past answers or see if a word has come up before? The Canuckle Archive has every puzzle back to launch. I use it when I'm convinced a word feels "too recent" — usually I'm wrong, but not always.

What to pay attention to

Canadian Wordle variants love their geography and bilingual terms, but the real pattern is letter distribution. Standard Wordle starter words (ADIEU, AROSE, STARE) still work, but Canadian content pulls from a smaller lexical pool. Words ending in -E or -Y are less common than in Wordle. You're more likely to see -T, -K, or -H endings.

Today's solve: I opened with CANOE (obvious, Canadian, got me the C and nothing else). Second guess was TRUCK — grabbed the T and U, missed everything else. That U-T combo narrowed things fast. Third guess was INUIT itself, but I hesitated because I thought "no way is it that straightforward." It was.

The Canuckle Solver Tool handles the Canadian word list if you want to check your remaining possibilities. I don't use it for the daily solve — kills the fun — but it's saved me on archive binges where I'm trying to reconstruct old puzzles.

One hard-won tip: when you have _NUI_ or similar patterns, don't overthink. Canuckle doesn't do obscure words. If it looks like a common Canadian term, it probably is.

Mistakes that cost guesses

I see three errors constantly, and I made two of them today:

Chasing the wrong vowel pattern. With I-N-U on the board, I spent a guess on INURE (valid Wordle word, not in Canuckle's list). The smaller answer pool means some standard Wordle guesses are dead ends. I should have committed to INUIT once the N and U were locked.

Ignoring the double-I possibility. I-I patterns are rare in English generally, but Canuckle has pulled INUIT, IMIDE, and a few others. My brain filters them out as "too weird." Don't let it.

Forgetting the thematic constraint. I threw in INPUT as a desperation guess. Technically valid, not remotely Canadian. The game doesn't reject non-Canadian guesses, but you're wasting rows if you ignore the theme.

My solve path today: CANOE → TRUCK → INUIT. Three guesses, but the third guess was pure luck that I didn't burn another on INPUT or INURE. The daily answer page updates at midnight EST if you ever need to verify or you're doing the puzzle late.

Questions players keep asking

Does Canuckle accept non-Canadian words as guesses?

Yes. The guess dictionary is standard English (roughly Wordle's list), but the answer list is curated Canadian. I've thrown in AMERICAN as a joke guess — it was accepted, gray tiles all the way. So you can use your normal starters, but don't expect AMERICAN to every be the solution. The thematic curation is real.

Why does INUIT keep coming up?

Because the answer pool is small and thematic. ~2,300 words sounds like a lot until you realize how many are geography (YUKON, ONTARIO), slang (TOQUE, HOSER, KEENER), and cultural terms. INUIT, METIS, CREE — these are high-frequency answers because they fit the constraints and the theme. I've seen INUIT three times since 2022. I'd bet METIS has appeared at least as often.

Is the Canuckle answer list public?

Not officially, but the community has reverse-engineered most of it through the API. The archive tracks confirmed answers. I've personally verified about 1,100 unique answers; the full list is probably discoverable if you scraped daily, but where's the fun in that?

What's the best starter for Canuckle specifically?

I rotate between CANOE, TRUCK, and HOSER. CANOE hits the Canadian theme hard (C, A, N, O, E are all common). TRUCK gets T, R, U, C, K — solid consonant coverage. HOSER is meme-y but efficient (H, O, S, E, R). AROSE and ADIEU still work fine, but I like leaning into the thematic words. Feels less generic.

My honest recommendation: pick one starter and stick with it. I switched too often in 2022 and my average guess count suffered. Consistency beats optimization when the word list is this constrained.
