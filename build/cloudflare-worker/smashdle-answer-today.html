Universe as your best filter

Smashdle uses the Super Smash Bros. Ultimate roster, and universe (the franchise a fighter comes from) is the single strongest attribute to filter on. Mario characters alone make up a significant chunk, but once you rule out one universe, you can focus your guesses on fighters from other franchises. If the answer is from a niche universe like Xenoblade or Fatal Fury, knowing your Nintendo history pays off.

The roster has roughly 80+ fighters including DLC. That's smaller than most other character-guessing games, but the attribute overlap between fighters from the same universe makes it tricky. Multiple Mario characters share similar weight classes and jump counts, so you need to dig deeper into specific attributes to split them.

Weight and jump count tricks

Weight class and number of jumps are the attributes most players ignore — and they shouldn't. Weight ranges from featherweight (Jigglypuff) to super heavyweight (Bowser), and it's a continuous scale, not just categories. If the answer is in the lightweight range, you can eliminate every heavyweight immediately.

Jump count is even more discriminating. Most fighters have 1 jump (plus their midair jump). Characters like Kirby, Meta Knight, and Pit have multiple midair jumps, which is rare. If the answer has 5+ jumps, you're looking at a very short list. This attribute alone can narrow things to under 10 fighters.

DLC fighters that stump people

DLC characters are the biggest source of wrong guesses. Fighters like Kazuya, Sephiroth, Pyra/Mythra, and Sora come from franchises that many Nintendo players don't follow closely. If you don't play Tekken, you won't know Kazuya's weight class or how many jumps he has. If you haven't played Kingdom Hearts, Sora's attributes are a mystery.

The solver handles this well. Enter what you got from your first guess — universe mismatch, weight class, jump count — and it filters the roster down to candidates that match. When the list hits single digits, you're almost there.

Yes — all DLC fighters from Fighters Pass Vol. 1, Vol. 2, and individual releases like Piranha Plant are in the pool.

What attributes does Smashdle use?

Universe, weight class, number of jumps, and other fighter-specific stats. Each attribute helps narrow the pool differently.

When the solver saves you time

Using a solver isn't about cheating; it's about managing your limited guesses when the roster exceeds 80 fighters. Early in a streak, you can afford to guess based on gut feeling. By day 15 or 20 of a month, or when the puzzle pulls an obscure alternate costume character, brute force fails. The solver helps you visualize the intersection of attributes. Instead of guessing "Cloud" and hoping, you input "Medium Weight" and "2 Jumps" to see exactly which fighters remain viable.

This approach prevents the common mistake of assuming a character's weight based on their visual design. Questions players keep asking

Does the game count alternate costumes as different fighters?

No, alternate costumes like Dark Samus or Alph are treated as distinct entries with their own stat lines, but standard color swaps for the same character do not count as separate answers. If the answer is Lucina, guessing a different color variant of Lucina won't work because the database treats them as the same entity. However, if the puzzle specifically features a character like Piranha Plant with different forms, those are usually consolidated under the main fighter entry unless specified otherwise in the daily notes.

Why did my guess for Marth fail if the stats matched?

If your stats for Universe, Weight, and Jumps matched Marth but the puzzle rejected it, you likely missed a subtle difference in the jump count or the specific game data source being used. Sometimes the database pulls from Smash Ultimate stats while your memory is from Smash 4. In Lucina's case, her weight is nearly identical to Marth's, but the jump count or a secondary attribute like "Ally" status might differ. Always double-check the specific attribute icons revealed after a wrong guess; they are the only source of truth.

How often do Fire Emblem characters appear as the answer?

Fire Emblem characters appear frequently due to the large number of representatives in the roster. Since the series has over a dozen fighters including the various lords and alchemists, statistically, you will encounter a Fire Emblem answer roughly once every week or two. If you see the universe clue early, don't panic. Narrow it down by checking who has appeared recently in the archive to avoid guessing someone who was the answer just three days ago.

Can I use the solver for past puzzles?

Yes, the solver logic applies to any date. If you are trying to complete a challenge or verify a past result, you can use the tool to reconstruct the logic path. The attribute data doesn't change day-to-day, only the target does. This is also a good way to learn the obscure stats of fighters you rarely play. Testing the solver against known answers from last month is a great way to calibrate your own internal database of fighter stats.
