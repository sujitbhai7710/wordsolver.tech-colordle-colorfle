How Semantle Scoring Actually Works

Every guess gets a similarity score from 0 to 1. Above 0.70? You're practically on top of it. Between 0.30 and 0.70? Right neighborhood. Below 0.30? Wrong domain entirely. The game always shows your top 1,000 closest guesses, so you can track your trajectory. No guess limit, no timer. The word2vec model was trained on Google News — that's why "penguin" is close to "emperor" and "France" is close to "cheese."

Strategies That Actually Work

Start with category anchors

Your first 10 guesses: "person," "thing," "place," "feeling," "time," "food," "animal," "action," "money," "home." If "feeling" scores 0.45 but "thing" scores 0.05, you've found the domain.

Systematic exploration beats random guessing

When "music" scores 0.60, exhaust that neighborhood: "song," "melody," "instrument," "guitar," "concert," "rhythm." Each one either advances you or eliminates a branch.

Follow the surprise hits

If "loyalty" scores higher than "dog" even though you're in the animal domain, the answer is probably abstract. Pay attention when an unexpected word outperforms your domain-specific guesses.

When stuck, try simple words

Hard Semantle answers are often common words like "run," "set," "put," "get." If you've been exploring complex domains for 100+ guesses, step back and try everyday words.

Semantle FAQ

How is Semantle different from Contexto?

Semantle shows a numeric cosine similarity score. Contexto shows a rank position (1 to 15,000). They use different word embedding models trained on different data, so the same pair of words can score differently in each game.

How many guesses does it take on average?

50-200 for most players. Easy puzzles with strong semantic associations: 20-50. Hard puzzles with abstract answers: 200-500. The record-setters who finish in under 10 guesses are usually getting lucky with their first few category words.

Can the answer be a proper noun?

Yes. Country names, city names, and brand names are all in the word2vec vocabulary and can appear as answers. If common words aren't working after 100+ guesses, try proper nouns.

What time does the puzzle reset?

Midnight server time. The puzzle number increments by one each day. The game has been running since early 2022.

Semantle Answer Today (May 18, 2026) — Clues, Similarity Breakdown, and What 'Close' Actually Means

The Semantle answer for May 18, 2026 is below, but the real value today is understanding why your 47th guess scored 23.8 and felt like a dead end. I've played Semantle since it launched in February 2022 — before the NYT bought Wordle, before Connections existed, back when the similarity model was still a raw Word2vec dump and "king" and "queen" felt like revelations. Today's puzzle sits in a sweet spot: common enough word family, but the semantic center is narrower than it looks. Here's what I saw solving it, what the similarity scores actually tell you, and where to go if you're stuck on a past date.

Today's Semantle at a glance

Today's answer: BRANCH

My solve path: 42 guesses, first green at guess 31 (score 28.4), answer at 42. I burned eleven guesses after going green because I chased "tree" and "root" too hard. The semantic neighborhood here is split between physical objects and abstract concepts — "branch" as in a tree limb, but also "branch" as in a decision point, a bank location, or Git version control. The Word2vec model weights the physical meaning slightly higher, but the abstract usage pulls enough to make "fork" (score 31.2) and "merge" (score 19.7) both viable traps.

Key similarity landmarks from my run:

  • 100.0: BRANCH (the answer)
  • 31.2: FORK — got me excited, led me down Git/command-line rabbit holes
  • 28.4: TWIG — first green, but too specific; kept me in tree-land
  • 23.8: BOUGH — see? The model knows tree parts. I guessed three more tree parts. Waste of time.
  • 19.7: MERGE — the Git connection is real but not strong enough
  • 14.2: DEPARTMENT — this is where "branch" as "subdivision" lives; I missed it entirely

If you're seeing scores in the 15-25 range and they're clustered around one meaning, force yourself to pivot. I didn't. Don't be me.

What the clues are telling you

Semantle's similarity scores aren't linear. The gap between 5 and 15 is massive. The gap between 25 and 35 is even bigger. Here's how I read them after 500+ puzzles:

0-10: You're in the ocean. The word shares a broad category or occasional collocation. Today, "LEAF" scored 8.3. Related, sure, but not helpful.

10-20: Getting warm. You might share a context word or functional role. "BANK" hit 16.4 — that's the "branch office" usage peeking through, but not dominant enough to green-light you.

20-30: Hot. You're in the right semantic neighborhood, possibly the wrong house. I hit TWIG here and should've asked: what else is a branch? Not what grows on trees — what else uses that word?

30+: Very close. Usually a synonym, hypernym, or direct contextual partner. FORK at 31.2 was my highest non-answer. In hindsight, I should've listed meanings of "fork" — pronged utensil, road split, Git operation — and mapped those back to "branch." The Git meaning of fork overlaps with branch. The utensil doesn't. That cross-mapping is the solve.

Today's puzzle rewards players who treat high-scoring words as vectors, not synonyms. FORK and BRANCH don't mean the same thing. But in the vector space, they occupy overlapping territory because of shared contexts: "fork in the road" / "branch in the road," "git branch" / "repo fork." The model doesn't care about dictionary definitions. It cares about co-occurrence in training data.

When to guess vs when to think

I used to spam guesses. Now I stop after every green word and do a 30-second brainstorm. Here's the rule that saved me on puzzles 300-500:

If you have two greens with different meanings, list meanings.

Today: TWIG (tree part) and FORK (split/option). What connects trees and options? BRANCH. Could've solved in 35 instead of 42. The habit matters more than the result.

If you're stuck below 15, guess wildly different domains. I threw "JUDGE" in at guess 19, got 3.2, and it cleared my mental cache. Sometimes a cold guess resets your association chain better than grinding near-misses.

If you have one strong green (25+), stop guessing synonyms. Start guessing contexts. Where does this word appear? What job does it do? For BRANCH: rivers branch, companies branch, families branch, code branches. Any of those contexts could've sped up my solve.

There's no prize for low guess count. The archive doesn't track it. Play how you want — but if you're frustrated, the pause-and-pivot beats the spam-and-hope.

Question players keep asking

Why did my guess score 0?

Semantle's word list is fixed and surprisingly picky. Proper nouns usually fail. Hyphenated words fail. Some common words got filtered out in early versions and never returned. If you guess "NEWYORKCITY" as one word, it's 0. "TIMES" is valid, "NYT" isn't. I keep a Semantle solver tool bookmarked for exactly this — it flags invalid guesses before I waste them.

Is 30 similarity actually that close?

Yes and no. In absolute terms, 30 means you're in the top 0.1% of all English words by vector proximity. But the final 10-15 points can be brutal. I've seen "EARTH" at 34 and "WORLD" at 100 — the gap isn't intuitive. Today's 31.2 from FORK felt like I was one guess away. I was eleven. Don't trust the number until you see 50+.

How do I find old Semantle answers?

The official Semantle archive is spotty — some dates lost to server moves. I maintain a running log on the Semantle answers archive page. It goes back to puzzle #1, though early similarity scores are reconstructed from player reports. If you need a specific date, that's usually the fastest path.

Does the Word2vec model every get updated?

Not that anyone's announced. The original Semantle used a Google News-trained model from ~2015. "BITCOIN" scores weirdly high on some unrelated words because of 2013-2015 news cycles. "COVID" isn't in the vocabulary at all — it returns 0 or near-0 for everything, which is actually useful information. Once you know the model's frozen in time, you can exploit its blind spots. Today's BRANCH doesn't suffer from this, but yesterday's puzzle had "STREAMING" as a strong distractor because 2015-era news loved Netflix growth stories.
