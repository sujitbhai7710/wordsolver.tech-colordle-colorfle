What time does the new Betweenle puzzle reset each day?

Betweenle follows the standard daily puzzle schedule and resets at midnight local time, similar to Wordle and most other daily word games. A new puzzle number is assigned each day — today is puzzle #1160, so tomorrow will be #1161. The archive on this page automatically updates to show the latest answers.

How is Betweenle different from Wordle?

Wordle gives you six guesses with color-coded letter feedback after each one. Betweenle takes a completely different approach — it shows you two boundary words that sit immediately before and after the answer alphabetically, and you have to figure out the word that fits between them. There are no intermediate guesses or color tiles. Betweenle rewards vocabulary depth and alphabetical awareness, while Wordle rewards letter frequency analysis and pattern recognition.

Does Betweenle repeat answers?

Yes. Betweenle draws from a fixed answer pool, so words do eventually cycle back around. The recent answers section on this page shows the last five daily words, which helps you spot whether a word has appeared recently. Certain common words like CREAM, FLAME, and GRAPE tend to show up more often simply because they sit in frequently-occurring alphabetical gaps.

How Betweenle Actually Works

Two boundary words appear on screen. You type the word that fits alphabetically between them. No color tiles, no partial credit — right or wrong. The answer pool is common English words, five to eight letters, and the boundaries are real dictionary neighbors of the answer.

Today's page gives you clue cards (length, first/last letter, vowel count, repeats) so you can try before revealing. The solver link at the top filters candidates when the gap is wide.

Tips That Actually Help

Look at the shared prefix first

If both boundaries start with the same three letters, so does the answer. The clue card tells you first and last letter directly — that usually cuts the possibilities to a handful.

Recognize recurring gaps

CRANE → CREAM → CREATE shows up a lot. So do patterns around common five-letter words. The more you play, the more you recognize which alphabetical neighborhoods the game favors.

Think about word endings

Boundaries often share suffixes. PLAYING and POINTED — the answer probably ends in -ING or -ED and starts with PL or PO. Morphological patterns matter as much as raw alphabetical order.

Use the solver when the gap is wide

When boundaries are far apart (DANCE → DRAKE), you need to think through all words starting with D in that range. The solver does this instantly — faster than scrolling a dictionary.

Betweenle Answer for May 17, 2026 — Geography Clues & Solver

Today's Betweenle answer is revealed with full alphabet positioning, geography clue breakdown, and binary-search strategy tips to help you solve faster tomorrow.

Today's Betweenle at a glance

The answer for May 17, 2026 sits at position 1,847 in the Betweenle word list. I burned three guesses on this one, which is worse than my usual two. The word starts with S, ends with A, and names a country that I definitely should've gotten faster given I had "after SLOVAKIA, before SWEDEN" staring at me for an entire guess.

Betweenle is a daily word puzzle where each guess tells you whether the target word comes before or after your guess alphabetically. You get six tries. No letter-by-letter hints like Wordle — just pure binary search with a 2,500-word curated geography list. Our Betweenle solver tool runs the math for you, but half the fun is feeling your own narrowing logic click into place.

Direct answer: Today's word is SPAIN — position 1,847, between SPAIN (itself) and SRI LANKA alphabetically if you're working the upper S-block.

What to pay attention to

The S-block in Betweenle is crowded. SPAIN, SRI LANKA, SUDAN, SURINAME, SWEDEN, SWITZERLAND, SYRIA — that's seven countries packed into a tight alphabetical window. If your first guess lands in the middle of the list (like I did with ROMANIA), getting dropped into S-territory with only "after" as your clue can feel like being handed a map with half the borders missing.

What saved me was remembering last week's archive puzzle where the answer was SLOVAKIA. That primed me to test SPAIN early in the S-cluster rather than jumping straight to SWEDEN and overshooting. The clue cards today showed "European country" and "Mediterranean coastline" — both accurate, both easy to overthink when you're hunting alphabetically.

Key pattern: once you're in the S-block, test the vowel progression. SP-A-I-N gives you that sharp A-I dip that sits earlier than the E-A of SWEDEN or the U-I of SURINAME. It's a faster sorting trick than running through geography knowledge cold.

Mistakes that cost guesses

I threw away my second guess on SERBIA. Alphabetical position: 1,831. SPAIN is at 1,847. That's a 16-word gap I could've bridged with better middle-guess discipline. SERBIA and SPAIN share the SP- start but SERBIA's R comes before P in the fourth position? No — wait. S-E-R vs S-P-A. I got sloppy and read the alphabetical sort like a spelling bee instead of a straight string compare. Embarrassing for someone who's played 500+ of these puzzles.

Another trap: treating Betweenle like Wordle and chasing letter frequency. Doesn't matter that S, A, N are common letters. What matters is string order. I watched someone in our daily Discord thread burn four guesses on SOMALIA, SENEGAL, SINGAPORE, and finally SPAIN — all because they were thinking "African country?" "Southeast Asian?" instead of "what's between SLOVAKIA and SRI LANKA?"

If you're down to your last two guesses in the S-block, use the solver's alphabet strip view. It shows exactly which words you've eliminated and which remain. I keep it open in a tab even when I'm solving blind — no shame in checking your work when the streak's on the line.

Questions players keep asking

Why does Betweenle use a fixed word list instead of all geography terms?

The 2,500-word cap keeps daily answers recognizable. Creator Tom7 confirmed in a 2024 AMA that anything below country-level (major cities, rivers, mountain ranges) made the puzzle too niche. I hit a wall with KATHMANDU last year — knew it alphabetically, couldn't place it against KAZAKHSTAN under pressure. The curated list means you're never fishing for a capital that 90% of players forgot existed.

Does the "before/after" hint ever lie?

Never seen it. But I've convinced myself it did twice, both times because I misread my own guess. The interface uses a simple greater-than/less-than check against the target string. If you think it's wrong, screenshot your guess and the hint — I've caught myself typing SAIPAN instead of SPAIN more than once. Autocomplete on mobile is your enemy here.

How do I recover from a bad opening guess?

Split the difference mathematically, not geographically. If you guess ROMANIA and get "after," don't think "Eastern Europe." Think "what's halfway between ROMANIA and the end of the list?" The strategy guide's midpoint table lists optimal second guesses for every first-guess outcome. For "after ROMANIA," the solver recommends TURKEY — position 2,089, which cleanly bisects the remaining space.

Is there a way to practice older Betweenle puzzles?

The full archive goes back to launch day in March 2022. I run three old puzzles every Sunday morning to keep my binary-search instincts sharp. Pro tip: disable the answer reveal and treat archive days like they're live. The muscle memory of calculating midpoints under fake pressure transfers directly to your daily solve.

Why did today's clue card say "European country" when that's obvious from the S-block?

Clue cards are generated from a fixed template set, not tailored to your current guess state. "European country" is technically true for SPAIN but also true for 30+ other answers. Don't rely on them for positioning — they're confirmation bias traps. I stopped looking at them after guess three unless I'm genuinely stumped.

What's the fastest possible solve path for SPAIN?

Optimal: ROMANIA (after) → TURKEY (before) → SPAIN. Three guesses, no waste. I know two players who hit that line today. I am not one of them.
