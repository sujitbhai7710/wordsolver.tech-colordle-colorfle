Editorial Policy

How WordSolverX verifies answers, sources data, reviews AI-assisted content, and corrects mistakes.

Answer Verification

We verify daily puzzle answers using each game's official source data or trusted API endpoints before publishing updates. For Wordle, we decode the official answer list embedded in the game's client-side JavaScript at build time, ensuring our data always matches the live game. For Nerdle, we pull from the equation database. For geography games like Worldle and Globle, we track the country selection algorithms. For character-guessing games such as LoLdle, Pokedle, and Smashdle, we maintain curated databases of all possible characters with their attributes, cross-referenced against the official game sources.

This automated verification process runs during each site build. The puzzle date calculation happens server-side, not in the browser, so the answer displayed on every page is baked into the HTML before it reaches you. When a game publisher modifies its answer list — as the New York Times did when removing certain words from Wordle — our build process picks up those changes immediately from the official source. We never rely on manual entry or user submissions for core answer data; the game itself is always the source of truth.

Methodology

Our answer-sourcing methodology depends on the type of puzzle, but the core principle is the same across every game: the answer comes directly from the official game source, not from speculation, community submissions, or AI generation. For games with embedded answer lists (Wordle, Quordle, Dordle, Octordle, Phrazle, Canuckle, and most Wordle variants), we extract the answer programmatically from the game's own JavaScript during our build process. For games with server-side APIs (Nerdle, Semantle, Contexto, Squaredle), we use automated scripts that fetch the daily answer at the appropriate rollover time and store it for our pages.

For character-guessing games, we maintain verified attribute databases. Each character entry — whether it's a League of Legends champion, a Pokémon, a Smash Bros fighter, or a Dota 2 hero — is manually compiled and periodically audited against the official game wikis and patch notes. When a game adds new characters or changes attributes through patches, we update our database within 24 hours. For geography games, we replicate the selection algorithm using the same seed data the games use. Every answer on this site is traceable back to its official source, and we document which method is used for each game type.

AI-Assisted Content

WordSolverX publishes daily answer pages for over 20 puzzle games. Each answer page includes not just the answer itself, but also a contextual article — hints, strategy tips, game mechanics explanations, and background information that helps players understand the puzzle better. These contextual articles are generated using AI language models as a drafting tool. We want to be transparent about this because honesty about how content is produced is fundamental to reader trust.

Here is exactly how AI is and is not used on this site: The daily answer for each puzzle — the word, character, country, or equation — is never determined by AI. Answers come exclusively from the official game sources described in our Methodology section above. What AI tools do is assist in writing the articles that accompany those answers: the paragraphs explaining today's hints, the strategy tips for approaching a specific puzzle, and the general-interest content that gives context to the answer. Every AI-generated article is reviewed and edited by our human editor, Preston Hayes, before publication. Preston checks each article for factual accuracy, ensures the hints and tips are correct for the specific day's puzzle, and rewrites any content that is vague, misleading, or inaccurate. If an AI-drafted article doesn't meet our quality standards, it is revised or replaced entirely before the page goes live.

We chose this approach because producing 20+ unique, detailed articles every day is beyond what a single editor can do from scratch while maintaining both quality and timeliness. AI drafting allows us to provide rich, contextual content on every answer page, while human editorial review ensures that content is accurate and genuinely useful. We believe this combination — machine speed for drafting, human judgment for quality control — serves readers better than either approach alone.

Content Standards

Every page on WordSolverX must meet specific quality criteria before it is published. These standards apply equally to AI-drafted and human-written content, and our editor enforces them during the review process. First, accuracy is non-negotiable: the answer displayed on each page must match the official game source, and any contextual information — hint descriptions, strategy advice, game mechanics explanations — must be factually correct for the specific puzzle date. We do not publish generic advice that could apply to any day; every article is tailored to the current day's answer.

Second, clarity and structure: the answer and essential puzzle information appear at the top of every page, immediately visible without scrolling. Optional hints, strategy content, and background articles follow below. A reader who only needs the answer should get it in under three seconds. A reader who wants more context should find well-organized, readable content below the fold. Third, no filler or fluff: articles must provide specific, actionable information. We avoid vague advice like "try common letters" in favor of concrete guidance like "for today's Wordle, the answer contains two vowels and starts with a consonant cluster that eliminates 40% of remaining possibilities." Fourth, readability: all content is written at a level accessible to a general audience, with clear headings, short paragraphs, and no unnecessary jargon. Technical details about game mechanics are explained in plain language.

Update Schedule

Most daily answer pages are refreshed at each game's rollover window, with core pages updated around midnight where applicable. Because we statically generate pages at build time rather than fetching answers dynamically in the browser, our update cycle is tied to our build and deployment process. For the majority of games, the answer becomes available in our system within minutes of the official game rollover, and the updated page is live within 15 minutes of the build completing.

Games that reset at midnight UTC — including Wordle, Quordle, Nerdle, and most Wordle variants — have their answer pages updated in our first build after midnight. Games with different reset times (some character-guessing games and geography games reset at different hours) are picked up in the next build cycle after their respective rollovers. For games that use server-side APIs rather than embedded answer lists, our automated fetch scripts run on schedules aligned with each game's specific reset time. We publish a "last updated" indicator on each answer page so you can verify freshness. If you notice a page that hasn't updated after a game's rollover, please let us know through the contact page and we'll investigate.

Corrections Policy

If an answer or solver result is incorrect, we treat it as a high-priority issue and correct it as quickly as possible after verification. Our correction process works like this: when a reader reports an error — or when we discover one ourselves — we first verify the claim against the official game source. If the answer on our page is indeed wrong, we correct the data, rebuild the affected page, and deploy the fix. For most corrections, the entire process takes under two hours during waking hours. For issues reported overnight, we typically resolve them by the following morning.

We also maintain a corrections log for transparency. When a published answer is corrected, we add a note to the page indicating what was changed and when. This applies to factual errors in answer data; it does not apply to edits that improve clarity, fix typos, or update contextual articles. We encourage readers to report any inaccuracies by including the page URL, the game name, the date in question, the answer we showed, and the correct answer. The more specific the report, the faster we can verify and fix it. Reports can be submitted via email to wordsolverx@gmail.com or through our contact page.

Contact for Corrections

If you find an incorrect answer, a broken solver, or any content that doesn't look right, we want to hear about it. The fastest way to reach us is by email at wordsolverx@gmail.com or through the contact page. When reporting an issue, please include the following details so we can investigate quickly: the page URL where you found the error, the name of the game, the date of the puzzle, the answer or content we displayed, and the correct answer or what you expected to see.

We review every correction request we receive. Not all reports result in changes — sometimes a game has multiple valid answers depending on region, mode, or time zone, and our page may be showing a correct answer that differs from what a player expected. But if there is a genuine error in our data, we will fix it and deploy the correction promptly. We also welcome feedback about our content more broadly: if an article is confusing, a hint is unhelpful, or a solver isn't working the way it should, let us know. Reader feedback directly improves the site.
